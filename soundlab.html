Phase 3L — the HRTF laboratory

Brian's ten-point review of this page as an HRTF demonstrator (ideas_crazy_7.txt) and his answers (ideas8.txt) — only the five items he bracketed. Each demo below stops every other one when it starts, and any demo that turns the listener puts it back facing forward on stop, since the AudioListener is global.

Position Explorer (Phase 3L, L.1)

One continuous sound, moved by fixed steps rather than a continuous orbit — the way to hear exactly what the spatializer can do, and the prototype of the galactic map's own sound cursor (A.13): steer a sound to a point, then "select" it. Start it, then put focus in the box: Left/Right rotate it around you 45° at a time, Up/Down move it 30° above or below, Page Up/Down move it 100 farther or closer, Shift plus any of those is the fine step (15°, 10°, 25), Home resets to dead ahead, Enter "selects" the current position, Escape stops.

Arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Home, and Enter work here once the explorer is running.

Flyby (Phase 3L, L.4)

Brian's eight audio/demo/propeller_plane1–8.mp3 recordings. Planes 1–4 loop on four different set routes around the listener; planes 5–8 are one-shot stunts, each fitted to its own length — a close pass, an overhead dive, a corkscrew, an underneath pass — cycling one at a time. No Doppler.

Spatial audio room (Phase 3L, L.8)

Five quiet, distinct, stationary sounds — Generator, Beacon, Crackle, Pump, Radio — placed randomly (position and distance) on every run. Start test solos each one in turn, in an order that is never the order of distance, then turns you 90° (announced first) and asks where things are now.

Press Start test.

Lighthouse gate (Phase 3L, L.9)

A directional source — PannerNode orientation and a sound cone, loudest when its beam points at you. This is the same voice and the same rotation the game's Jump Gate now uses (space_station6, a 10-second sweep).

Left/Right and Page Up/Down work here once the lighthouse is running.

Lock tone candidates (SPEC 2.11)

The problem: a lock in combat lasts seconds and the solid 880 Hz tone suits it; a lock on a sector beacon or a mining rock can last a whole flight, and the same tone is harsh over minutes. Combat keeps the solid tone either way. These three are candidates for what sector and mining get instead. Each plays for a few seconds then stops itself.

3D vortex (ideas6, reshaped by Phase 3L, L.5)

Brian's eight vortex recordings (audio/quadrant/vortex), each looping on its own HRTF panner and orbiting the listener at its own radius and speed — four one way, four the other. As of L.5 each vortex has its own vertical sway, centre height, and orbit offset, shaped one at a time. Start it, then put focus in the box: Up/Down select a vortex, Page Up/Down change its sway, Home/End change its centre height, [ and ] slide its orbit centre toward or away from you, Left/Right change the shared speed for all eight, R resets every vortex, Escape stops. The status line reads back the selected vortex after every change.

Arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Home, End, [ and ], and R work here once the orbit is running.

On disk, not in the manifest
